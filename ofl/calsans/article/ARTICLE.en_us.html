Cal Sans is a geometric sans-serif typeface designed for product interfaces and brand work alike. Commissioned by Peer Richelsen for Cal.com and designed by Mark Davis of WORDMARK, it started life as a single, bold display style and has grown into a complete variable family, with weights from Regular to Bold, matching italics, and an optical size axis that adapts the design from fine print to large headlines.

To contribute, see github.com/calcom/sans.

Cal Sans is drawn as a system rather than a single shape scaled up and down. Its small and large optical sizes are separate drawings: as the size decreases, corners soften, overshoots relax and spacing opens up, so that labels and captions stay clear while headlines stay tight and confident. Turn on optical sizing and the font picks the right drawing for each size on its own.

Its geometry comes from the tradition of Futura. Where most digital revivals flattened Paul Renner’s design into one drawing, Cal Sans returns to the original discipline of adjusting the letters for each size, while keeping the pure circles and clean lines that give the style its character. The forms were tested by blurring them, to simulate tired eyes, poor light or a distant screen, and refined until words held together.

At the heart of the family is the Geometric Form axis (GEOM). Sliding it moves the letterforms from an accessibility-first design, with clearly distinguished shapes for the hardest reading conditions, through a calm interface style, to the classic Cal Sans, and finally to a fully geometric set inspired by the display types of Ed Benguiat and Paul Renner. Letters switch cleanly from one form to the next along the way, making four distinct typefaces available in one font.

Two additional axes give finer control for display work: Ascender Height (YTAS) raises the ascenders for taller, airier headlines, and Sharpness (SHRP) gives corners a crisper finish. The italics are true italics, drawn at 9.5° with the help of Sebastian Carewe’s Italify, rather than slanted versions of the upright.

At text sizes, Cal Sans is built to slot into existing interfaces. Its capitals and lowercase sit in the same space as the system and open-source fonts interfaces already use, and its line spacing is balanced so that labels sit in the optical center of buttons, badges and inputs without any manual adjustment. For product UI, the companion family Cal Sans Text UI offers the interface design ready to use, tuned for small sizes.

To recreate the look of the original Cal Sans, set the weight to Bold and the optical size to its largest value.
