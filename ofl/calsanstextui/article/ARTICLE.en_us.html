Cal Sans Text UI is a geometric sans-serif designed for product interfaces: buttons, labels, menus, forms and the small text that fills every screen. It is the interface companion of Cal Sans, the variable family commissioned by Peer Richelsen for Cal.com and designed by Mark Davis of WORDMARK. Cal Sans Text UI comes in weights from Regular to Bold, with matching italics.

To contribute, see github.com/calcom/sans.

Cal Sans covers a wide range of uses in one variable font, from accessibility-first letterforms to fully geometric display shapes, and from fine print to large headlines. Cal Sans Text UI takes one precise point in that range and makes it ready to use: the calm interface letterforms of Cal Sans, in the version drawn for small sizes. There are no axes to set and no settings to remember; the font simply works at the sizes interfaces use most.

The small-size drawing is what makes the difference. Compared with the display styles of Cal Sans, its corners are softer, its details sturdier and its spacing more open, so words stay clear and even at 12 or 14 pixels. Apertures are kept wide, so letters like a, c, e and s remain distinct even when text is read quickly, on a distant screen or in poor light.

Some letters are adjusted for clarity in interface text. The lowercase l has a small tail, so it is never confused with a capital I or the number 1 in usernames, codes or passwords. The ascenders are slightly taller than in the default Cal Sans, which helps word shapes stay recognizable at small sizes.

Cal Sans Text UI is also built to slot into existing designs. Its capitals and lowercase sit in the same space as the system fonts interfaces already ship with, so it can replace them without resizing components. Its line spacing is balanced around the capitals, so a label sits in the optical center of a button, badge or input field without any manual adjustment.

For headlines, branding and display sizes, or to explore the other styles of the family, use Cal Sans, which shares the same design and pairs naturally with Cal Sans Text UI.
